An expert, here, is any component that can do a task: an AI model, or a few lines of plain rules.

Concept 1 · mixture of experts

Specialists plus a gate that picks one for each input

In machine learning, a mixture of experts combines specialist sub-models with a gate that sends each input to the right one (Jacobs et al., 1991). Large modern models use it so that only a few experts work on each word (Shazeer et al., 2017).

Example 1 · everyday

Hospital triage: a nurse sends a sprained ankle to the walk-in clinic and chest pain to the cardiologist. Nobody sees every specialist.

Example 2 · the project

The same idea across whole models: a router with readable rules sends sorting to Jev and number-finding to Claude.

Mixture of Experts · 3 / 9

Be precise with students: inside a large model the gate is learned, not written by hand, and nobody can read its reasons. The project borrows the idea at the application level, where the gate is plain rules a person can read and every choice is logged with its reason.

Concepts 2 and 3 · escalation and fallback

Go up for hard cases; go down when a model is missing

Escalation: to a stronger expert

Everyday: a help desk passes a hard question to a senior engineer.

Project: Haiku marks its extraction "low confidence", so Sonnet tries again; both calls are logged.

Fallback: to a simpler expert

Everyday: during a phone outage, staff answer from a printed FAQ.

Project: Jev unreachable, so Claude answers the same questions; no Claude key, so keyword rules guess and a person reviews.

Together they give graceful degradation: the agent keeps working at lower quality instead of stopping, and says so in the log.

Mixture of Experts · 6 / 9

Escalation spends more only on the few hard cases. Fallback keeps the pipeline alive during an outage. In both cases a person can see from the log that something unusual happened, and every rules guess on a real note is sent to the review queue.

Concept 4 · caching

Never pay twice for the same answer

58%

of 1,294 model calls in the reference run were answered from the cache, at $0. The whole run cost $0.12.

A cache stores each answer under a fingerprint of the question: task, model, prompt version and note.

Everyday: writing a friend's number in your contacts instead of asking every time.

Project: "Fair Trade + organic certified" appears in many imports; Jev is asked once.

Mixture of Experts · 7 / 9 · model_usage_log, reference run 2026-10-13

The fingerprint includes the prompt version, so changing a question automatically stops old cached answers being reused. Cost per expert in the reference run: Jev $0.008, Claude Haiku $0.045, Claude Sonnet $0.070. Sonnet ran only 9 times yet cost the most, which shows why the router keeps it for rare cases.

Concept 5 · an accountable router

Every routing decision leaves a row anyone can read

Example 1 · everyday

A receptionist's call sheet: time, caller, who the call went to, and why. A complaint can be traced to the right desk.

Check your understanding

Three questions

  1. How does the project's router differ from the gate inside a large mixture-of-experts model?
  2. The daily budget runs out at 2 p.m. Where do the afternoon's notes go, and what does a person see?
  3. Why are the guards checked before the expert rules, not after?

Mixture of Experts · 9 / 9

Answers. 1: the gate inside a model is learned and cannot explain itself; the router is a short list of rules a person wrote, and each choice is logged with its reason. 2: to the keyword rules; a budget incident appears in incident_log, rules guesses go to the review queue, and model_usage_log shows "daily budget reached". 3: so that no rule can call a paid model once the AI is switched off or the budget is spent.
